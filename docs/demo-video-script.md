# FairChange video script

This is the voiceover handoff for the premium 1080p judging video. The judging cut is designed for a **3:15 runtime** at 30 fps. Record one take per scene and one continuous take at the same pace; the edit can replace either version without changing the motion design.

## Full judging cut — 3:15

| Time | Scene | Voiceover | On-screen direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| 00:00–00:12 | The request | “A client asks for one more change. The team needs to know whether it is a defect, included work, or a new commercial commitment.” | Open on the UK and Canada request card. Animate the three possible outcomes as a quiet decision fork. |
| 00:12–00:28 | The hidden cost | “That answer is usually scattered across a signed scope, old correspondence, estimates, and delivery tasks. FairChange brings those records together before anyone promises the next step.” | Records drift in from the edges and resolve into one evidence trail. |
| 00:28–00:45 | The promise | “It automates the repetitive search and explanation, while keeping the final commercial decision with the people who own it.” | Logo mark resolves into the line: **Automation handles the search. People own the commitment.** |
| 00:45–01:05 | The flow | “The workflow is simple: take in the request, retrieve the relevant evidence, classify what changed, and route the result through the right human gate.” | Animate **Intake → Evidence → Decision → Human gate** with the FairChange mark moving between stages. |
| 01:05–01:24 | Defect | “First, an inactive representative is receiving qualifying US leads. The signed scope requires active-representative routing, and acceptance failures are corrected without an extra fee. FairChange calls this a defect and prepares a non-billable corrective task.” | Show Case 1, highlight S1 and S5, then land on **Defect**. |
| 01:24–01:43 | Included revision | “Next, a client asks to move two existing dashboard charts. One revision round remains in scope, so this is included work. The safe action is to update the existing delivery task.” | Show Case 2, highlight S2, then land on **Included revision**. |
| 01:43–02:08 | Scope change | “The third request asks for UK and Canada routing and suggests trading unstarted training for it. Additional countries are excluded, and the proposed substitution changes the commercial agreement. FairChange classifies this as a scope change.” | Show Case 3, highlight S3 and S4, then land on **Scope change**. |
| 02:08–02:30 | Human gate | “The agent does not approve that change. It prepares an evidence-backed proposal. An owner reviews the exact version, the client accepts the exact proposal, and only then can the scope revision and delivery task move forward.” | Animate **Owner review → Client acceptance**. Let the gate hold for a beat before the next scene. |
| 02:30–02:50 | Product walkthrough | “In the workspace, the evidence comes before the answer. A judge can select each case, inspect the source trail, read the next safe action, and open the reasoning behind the decision.” | Use a polished browser-style view of the live public workspace. Select all three cases with cursor focus. |
| 02:50–03:05 | Independent session | “The separate session kit lets an outside participant classify the same fictional cases independently. It creates an anonymous report locally and never sends customer data or credentials anywhere.” | Show the session kit, its glossary, three cards, and local report state. |
| 03:05–03:15 | Close | “FairChange makes scope decisions explainable before they become commercial surprises.” | Logo, repository URL, public demo URL, and **Professional Agents** track. |

### Recording notes

- Speak conversationally and leave a half-second pause at each scene boundary.
- Aim for a calm, confident pace of roughly 135–145 words per minute.
- Emphasize **defect**, **included work**, **scope change**, and **human gate**.
- Record clean mono or stereo WAV at 48 kHz if possible; keep the room quiet and leave a few seconds of room tone at the start and end.
- Do not read the on-screen labels that are already obvious; the narration should explain why each outcome is safe.
