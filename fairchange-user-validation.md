# FairChange — user validation kit

**Target:** Two or three independent GTM consultants, CRM implementers or small agency owners. Fifteen minutes each. No participants recruited or feedback collected yet.

## Initial ICP and recruitment

Our first user is a consultant or delivery lead who implements CRM, lead routing, reporting or workflow automation for clients under a defined scope. Our initial buyer hypothesis is the owner of a solo practice or a 2–20-person consultancy. Team size is a targeting hypothesis, not a verified attribute of the candidates below. Prioritize people who personally decide whether a request is included, a defect or an extra.

The founder has no warm tester contacts. Start with a small set of personalized invitations, with founder approval of recipients and sending channel. Expand toward 8–12 relevant invitations if needed; there is no assured response rate. Ask for a short review by September 12 and offer an asynchronous three-case review as an alternative. No follow-up after a decline.

## Invitation draft — not sent

“I'm building a tool that checks client change requests against the agreed scope, distinguishes fixes from extra work, and prepares the next steps. Could you spend 15 minutes trying a fictional CRM project and telling me where it gets the judgment wrong? No client data needed. This is an early prototype, not a sales call.”

The founder can send this to suitable contacts. Codex will send nothing without explicit authorization identifying recipients or an agreed audience/channel.

## Session

1. Ask permission to keep anonymized notes and timings. Ask separately before using any attributed quote. Record whether the participant is a contact, prospective user or founder; do not obscure that relationship.
2. Give the participant a fictional scope, relevant correspondence and a request. Ask them to determine what is owed and prepare a response using their normal approach. Time the task.
3. Give them an equivalent case through FairChange. Time review and completion. Vary case order across participants to reduce learning effects.
4. Ask: “What did it misunderstand?”, “Would you send this response?”, “What evidence is missing?”, and “Which part would actually remove work from your day?”
5. Ask whether they would try it on another anonymized example. An agreement is pilot interest, not a sale.

## Record per session

- Participant code and role; relationship to founder; consent scope.
- Date, case IDs, task order and prototype version.
- Baseline and assisted completion times; whether each task was completed correctly.
- Incorrect classifications, unfair charges, missing evidence and edits required.
- Exact feedback, with attribution permission recorded separately.

Report the small sample honestly. Do not extrapolate annual savings from a few fictional tasks. If external testers are unavailable, publish founder walkthrough results and synthetic evaluations under those labels; independent validation remains incomplete.

## Founder input that improves the cases

Useful examples: a request that looked additional but had already been promised; a defect someone tried to charge for; or an extra request resolved by trading an existing deliverable. Summarize without client names or confidential material. Founder examples inform design; they do not count as independent endorsements.
